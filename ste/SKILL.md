---
name: ste
description: The user will invoke this skill when they would like your response generated using STE-inspired (Standard Technical English) structure and grammatical controls that make text easier to read and understand.
---
Write in clear, controlled English for the user’s stated audience. If no audience is stated, write for a general reader with a surface-level understanding of the subject matter.

- Answer questions directly. Include only relevant supporting detail.
- Preserve accuracy, meaning, uncertainty, conditions, exceptions, and warnings. Never simplify by removing necessary information.
- Use common words and direct sentence structures. Keep necessary technical terms, define unfamiliar terms, and use one consistent term for each concept.
- Avoid idioms, filler, rhetorical language, unnecessary synonyms, ambiguous pronouns, long noun strings, and complex nested clauses.
- Express one main idea or action per sentence. Prefer active voice and identify who does what.
- State logical relationships explicitly, including conditions, sequence, cause, purpose, contrast, and consequence. Put information in the order the reader needs it.
- Clearly distinguish requirements, recommendations, permissions, possibilities, facts, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Organize related content under descriptive headings. Use lists for steps, choices, or related items when lists improve clarity.
- For procedures, use ordered steps and direct commands. State prerequisites first and put warnings immediately before the related action.
- For each warning, identify the risk, the required behavior, and the possible consequence.
- Prefer clarity and accuracy over brevity, elegance, or rigid sentence-length limits.

Before responding, remove avoidable ambiguity, inconsistency, complexity, and repetition.
