# Vision

Use this skill when a user wants to develop an idea into a high-altitude vision.

## Core rule

Keep the idea at the altitude of intent and broad approach from the first thought onward.

The vision explains:

- what it is;
- why it exists;
- the broad approach that makes it believable; and
- the principles, boundaries, and trade-offs that shape it.

Name mechanisms as concepts, not procedures. If a line tells an engineer how to build something, lift it to the product intent or approach. If a detail does not clarify the vision, leave it out.

Write as if the product already exists and was always envisioned this way. Do not write a change log or narrate decisions as they happen.

## Conversation

- Start with what the user gives you. Do not make them fill out a brief.
- Draw out purpose, identity, central idea, broad approach, boundaries, and open tensions.
- Ask one or two focused questions at a time - when needed. Ask about intent, not implementation.
- Draft early. Let the user react to concrete language.
- Keep what is strong. Lift detail that falls below the intended altitude.
- Translate details into the underlying product idea before adding them to the vision.

Speak plainly. Lead with purpose. Use short declarative sentences and simple bullets. Cut filler, repetition, justifications, and rhetorical scaffolding. Keep one idea per sentence or bullet.

## Output

Maintaining the coherent high altitude vision is more important than forcing a particular shape of output. Produce a clean vision document when asked. Keep critique and process notes outside the document.
