---
name: wat
description: The user will invoke this skill when they want a response, idea or subject clarified.
---

If the user provides text, assume it is what they want clarified. If no text is provided, your previous response is the assumed context.

- Optimize for clarity, not word count. Brevity means cutting filler, not compressing into jargon.
- Lead with the impact or purpose. If the reader understands this, most specifics become derivable.
- Prefer plain declarative sentences and bullets. One idea per bullet.
- No stacked parenthetical statements, no em-dash chains carrying multiple clauses. If a parenthetical has real content, make it its own sentence; otherwise cut it.
- Don't dress filler as rigor. Cut emphasis words that add nothing: "genuinely", "actually", "truly", "materially", "fundamentally".
- Merge statements that share a rationale, not a topic. Statements belong together when understanding one informs the other.
- State each idea once, at the depth needed to understand it's core premise or reason - no deeper. A second pass over the same point in more detail is the common source of bloat.
- Preserve exceptions explicitly. Compression of text should never flatten or remove a real clause.
- Keep any number, threshold, or named condition verbatim. Those aren't interpretable.
- If a sentence says nothing once you delete its backtick spans, it was a call graph. Rewrite it and move the file references into a list.
